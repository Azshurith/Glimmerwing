Glimmerwing

Play online

How to win. Pop a dandelion puff and its pollen burst pops its neighbours. Chains of three or more send falling stars into your rival’s meadow; seven or more add a thorny bramble. Popping critters fills your power gauge (bottom of the meadow) through three levels. Hold fire to charge, one level at a time as far as the gauge allows, and let go: level 1 fires a piercing torpedo that clears a lane; level 2 also sends your fairy’s Extra Attack, a few small critters that hunt your rival (costs half a level); MAX summons your guardian into their meadow (costs two levels). With the gauge below level 1 you can’t charge at all. Breaking three or more reverse stars in one chain also sends your guardian. Guardians drift in and shadow you, lock in place to attack, then sink out through the bottom.

Bat stars back. A star’s colour shows the hits it needs: rose takes four, orange three, gold two, pale one. Break a plain star and it flies back to your rival as a flashing reverse star, tougher. Break a reverse star and it turns into one of your Extra Attack critters instead. Most stars drop straight, but watch for seekers, which slide over you and then plunge (a faint line shows where), and slanted stars that cut diagonals and glance off the walls. The longer a round runs, the faster everything comes at you (watch the tempo in the corner), and each new round of a match starts faster. Lose all five hearts and you lose the round; best of three takes the match.

Fever. About half a minute in, “Moonseed rising” flashes and a pale crescent orb drifts in with the next puffs. Shots bounce off it; catch it in a pollen burst to break it. That starts Fever: a spread shot, a rush of puffs, a gauge that fills twice as fast, and every chain sends one star per pop after the first, so a 5-chain sends four.

Tough puffs and late rounds. A puff’s seed tips show the shots it takes, dropping a step with each hit: moonsilver five, amber four, sky three, mint two, cream one. Any pollen burst pops it outright. Some waves come wrapped in dew bubbles that pop first, big puffs leave extra-wide bursts, and watch for waves that sneak up from below. Beetles cut diagonals inside a glassy shell that has to break first; spinners corkscrew down. Waves also swoop in from a corner, sweep across in a line, or drop in squeezing columns, and some ring a tough leader with small fry: pop the leader to clear the lot. Tougher critters leave bigger bursts. Waves don’t run on a clock: the next one comes in as soon as the last is popped or gone, so clearing fast brings the swarm faster. At about a hundred seconds every critter speeds up sharply, and soon after a hollow crow wakes in one meadow. It circles and dives at the fairy, then crosses into the other meadow, back and forth. Shoot it enough to drive it off and it reappears in your rival’s meadow, sooner and faster each time.

Each fairy fights her own way. Pip’s charge is a steady Sunorb, Bramblewick’s a slow, heavy Thornburst, Wisp fans out three Frost needles, Clove fires Twin darts, Sorrel’s Ember fan is a short-range cone, and Umbra’s Dusk crescent sweeps a wide arc. Each has her own Extra Attack, guardian and bloom.

Petal bloom. Two per round, shown as flowers by your hearts. A bloom sweeps your meadow clear of bullets and incoming stars, pops small fry, stings a guardian, and makes you untouchable for two seconds. It never starts a chain or sends anything, so it’s for getting out of trouble, not scoring.

Fairies. Three fairies are ready from the start. Win matches, chain big, and beat the CPU cleanly to unlock three more. Progress is saved in this browser.

Controls. Three buttons, like the arcade: A shoots (tap) or charges (hold and let go), B sets off a petal bloom, C autofires while held. Vs CPU and online: arrows or WASD to move, A is Z or Space, B is X, C is C. Two players: WASD with G to shoot and charge, F to autofire, H to bloom (these work vs CPU too); arrows with Enter, right Shift, '. Gamepads work too: stick or d-pad to move, A/B as marked, X or Y to autofire, Start to confirm; the first pad is player one, the second player two. On touch, drag to steer and hold to charge; tap a second finger to bloom. P pauses.

Online. Press Play online (or 3). Find match pairs you with the next player looking for a game; Create friend room gives you a four-letter code for a friend to join. Online you use the single-player controls, and your meadow is always on the left. Esc between rounds leaves.
